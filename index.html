Four colour systems.
Now one.

A new token model to set the foundations for colour on the apps: one system — primitives composing semantic roles, one spelling per job — shared by humans and agents, correct in light and dark.

01

Four systems competing for every colour

HHColors, the asset catalogue, raw SwiftUI — and ComponentsKit, an abstraction layer sitting on top of the native theme. None was authoritative, and 9 names were defined twice.

02

Hard-coded values, orphans everywhere

An explosion of colours typed by hand: 133 tokens spelling 144 distinct hex literals, 61 of which sit on no ramp — one-offs that exist nowhere else in the product.

03

No primitives, no shared source of truth

Nothing composed the values and nothing was shared across platforms — iOS, web and Android each spelled colour their own way.

04

Semantic layers without real roles

Names never said what they painted — background competed with surface, outline with border — so humans and agents applied them inconsistently.

05

Dark theme applied inconsistently

Light and dark were kept in step by hand: fill stops painted text, alpha dropped through the bridge, and every defect hid in the theme you weren't looking at.

All of it made colour — the foundation of the interface — impossible for us and the agents to apply with one coherent strategy.
